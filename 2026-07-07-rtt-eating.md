# RTT: Your Body Already Knows. You Keep Lying to Yourself.

*Real Talk Tuesday — July 2026*

---

### The Story

There was a bag of Sour Patch Kids on the counter.

Not mine. One of the kids left it there.

For four hours on a Sunday afternoon, I walked past it every time I went to the kitchen. Pour coffee. Walk past. Refill water. Walk past. Put a dish away. Walk past.

By the third lap, I wasn't thinking about the candy.

I *was* thinking about the candy.

That's how it starts. Not a binge. Not some big deliberate moment. Just proximity. And a permission slip your brain quietly writes for itself when you're tired, you haven't eaten right, and you just need to get through the afternoon.

Here's the thing: I know what Keto does for me.

The energy is steady. The fog lifts. I don't crash at 2 PM like I'm running on fumes. I don't wake up with wet sand in my skull. I've lived it. I have the data.

And still — the candy called.

That's not a willpower failure. That's a logistics failure.

When the system is solid — meals planned, fridge stocked, protein and fat already in your body — the candy on the counter is background noise. You don't even see it.

But when the system breaks down? When you skipped breakfast and it's 3 PM and you're running on caffeine and spite? That's when Sour Patch Kids stop looking like junk food.

They look like relief.

---

### The Gear

This is the verse that's been living rent-free in my head:

*"Whatever you eat or drink, do it for the glory of God."* — 1 Corinthians 10:31

I used to read that as a gratitude thing. Say grace. Be thankful. Move on.

But I don't think that's it.

I think it's about stewardship.

Your body is the one piece of equipment you've been given to do this whole life with. To lead at work. To come home and actually be there. To get on the floor with your kids. To still have something left to give at 9 PM when your wife needs you.

If you're fueling yourself with junk all day, you are showing up everywhere at 60%.

60% at work.

60% at home.

60% as a husband.

60% as a dad.

That's not noble. That's just a broken system with a convincing excuse.

For me, Keto isn't about discipline. It's about showing up whole. When I'm dialed in, I'm a different man. That's not a feeling — that's just the truth of what clean fuel does to a body built for work.

---

### The Move

You already know.

You don't need me to convince you. You've had the two clean weeks. You remember exactly how that felt. You also know what drift looks like — tired by noon, foggy by 3, useless by 7.

The question isn't whether you know.

The question is whether you'll build something that makes it easy to do what you already know is right.

Three moves. That's the whole reset.

**1. Test it honestly.** Not what Instagram says. Not your coworker's opinion. *Your* energy. *Your* sleep. *Your* clarity. Run two intentional weeks and let your body give you the answer. You'll know.

**2. Stock the kitchen for the right version of yourself.** Not the 9 PM tired version who needs a quick win. The version you want to be tomorrow morning. If the junk is in the house, you will eat it eventually. Not because you're weak — because proximity always wins.

**3. Play the long game.** Every meal either buys you capacity or costs you capacity. That's the whole ledger. No guilt. No spiral. Just the accounting.

Walk past the candy.

Not because you're disciplined.

Because you've built a life worth showing up for — and you need to be fully *on* to do it.

Do it for the glory of God.

— Christopher
