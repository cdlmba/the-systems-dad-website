# Social Copy Pre-Flight Filter

## WHEN TO USE THIS SKILL
Load this skill at the START of every session that involves writing or rewriting social media copy.
Run the checklist below on EVERY draft line by line before it is shown to the user.
If a draft fails any check, fix it silently and re-run the check. Do not show the raw draft.

## STEP 2: SENTENCE LENGTH & UNIVERSAL 5TH-GRADE TEST
Read every sentence. Count the words.

- Target: 8-12 words per sentence.
- Max: 15 words.
- Any sentence over 15 words must be cut in half.
- Universal Domain Coverage: This reading-level ceiling applies to ALL topics—including creative work, age/career transitions, modern technology, and vintage/philosophical adaptations. Never allow topic shifts to justify college-level vocabulary or compound clauses.

Test: If a tired coworker would not say this exact sentence out loud to a 5th grader across a desk, rewrite it.

---

## STEP 3: STRUCTURE TEST
Check each of the following. Fix before output.

- No bullet points. Convert all bullet lists to consecutive plain sentences.
- No triads. No three-part lists (X, Y, and Z). Break into two items or one direct statement.
- No em-dash stitching. No compound sentences fusing two ideas with a dash. Split into two sentences.
- No 1-line drop stacks on LinkedIn. For LinkedIn, avoid five or more consecutive single-sentence paragraphs (group into 2-sentence micro-blocks). NOTE: For X / DadOps narrative posts, single-line staccato beats with double line breaks are explicitly permitted and preferred for high emotional tension.
- No negative parallelisms. No "Not X, but Y" or "Less X, more Y." State the positive directly.
- No dangling -ing closers. No "...reflecting its broader impact." Cut the gloss; state the fact.
- No keynote bumper stickers. No "Real leadership is not X; it is Y." End on the boring, physical, concrete result.
- No formulaic timestamp hooks. Strictly avoid opening posts with repetitive "[Day] at [Time]" templates. Vary entries: physical object in hand, direct dialogue, observable absurdity, or in medias res.
- The Hard Stop (No Editorial Tails). Strictly ban tacking on 1–2 final sentences that explain the lesson, moral, or broader significance (e.g., "And that is how you reclaim your life" or "Older wisdom still holds"). Cut the final beat. End cold on the physical object, the binary constraint, or the unvarnished factual outcome. If the last sentence explains what the post just showed, delete it.

---

## STEP 4: ANTI-POETIC ENFORCEMENT & JARGON SMELL TEST
Read the full draft as if you are presenting it to a bored warehouse worker at 4:45 PM on a Friday.

Ask: Does any sentence sound like a consultant trying to seem smart?
Ask: Does any sentence sound like a cinematic voiceover or emotional poetry? (e.g., "running the meeting in my head," "let the day drain out," "used it all up")

If yes, replace it with what that consultant would say after three beers. Plain, direct, slightly tired.
For emotional scenes, replace all poetic metaphors with boring, literal physical reality (e.g., "head still at work," "I sit there and do nothing for ten minutes," "I have nothing left").

---

## STEP 5: PHYSICAL OBJECT & SPATIAL REALITY TEST
Every post must contain at least one physical, touchable object.

Examples: phone, charger, laptop, paper calendar, kitchen counter, car, red block on a screen, Slack message, missed date in red, shared project card.

Abstract concepts must be anchored to a physical moment or object. If the draft has no physical object, add one.

Spatial Reality Check: Ensure domestic geography is 100% physically coherent. Never blend home-office mechanics (steps to the kitchen, door threshold) with corporate commute mechanics (driveway, car, highway). If the narrator is working from home, the boundary is the office threshold, not the car.

---

## STEP 6: POV CONSISTENCY TEST (X / DadOps posts only)
Pick one and stay in it for the entire post.

- First-person "I" for personal anecdotes (failure, fix, what I did).
- Second-person "You" for universal operator scenarios.

Do NOT switch between "I" and "You" mid-post.

---

## STEP 7: FINAL READ-ALOUD CHECK
Read the entire draft aloud, start to finish, in a normal speaking voice.

Any sentence you stumble on or that sounds robotic must be rewritten.

Only after completing all 7 steps may the draft be shown to the user.
