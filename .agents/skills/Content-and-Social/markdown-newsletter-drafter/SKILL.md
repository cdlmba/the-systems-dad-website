# Systems Dad Newsletter Drafter (Kit / PFR Edition)

Use this skill to draft the "Systems Dad" newsletter. Every draft must run through the **Pain-First Rewrite (PFR)** process to ensure it resonates with busy fathers while maintaining technical authority.

## 🔒 The Locked-In Skill: “Pain-First Rewrite” (PFR)

Follow this 4-step process for every newsletter draft:

### Step 1: Pain-First Opening (Chaos – 1-2 paragraphs)
Start with a specific, emotional dad moment where **you** were the problem. Use “I” language and concrete details (7:55 AM yelling, dinner table guilt, WiFi fights). 
- **Rule**: Make it sting. Make the reader think “Damn… that’s me.” 
- **Rule**: No systems talk, no metaphors, no “you are no different” until the pain is felt.

### Step 2: Operator Bridge (Your Skill Transfer)
Transition with your program manager / corporate background. Show how you applied work skills to your own home chaos (humbly, not as a flex). 
- **Bridge clearly**: “So I did what I do at the office…” or “As a PM who organizes chaos at work, I…” 
- Introduce the tactic/system as something you built **because of the pain**, not as a shiny protocol. Keep it to 1-3 simple, actionable steps.

### Step 3: Human Closer (Vulnerability Anchor)
Always end the story with imperfection: “It helps most weeks/days,” “I still slip,” “I still apologize,” “Still figuring it out.”
- This prevents guru vibes and keeps dads trusting you. Relatability is the currency.

### Step 4: Newsletter Jump (Soft Invitation)
One short paragraph that says: “If this hit you, here’s more of the real/messier version in this newsletter…”
- Promise ongoing honesty (wins, spectacular fails, small tweaks).
- End with a low-pressure reply prompt tied to their pain (e.g., “What’s the #1 thing still leaking into your family time?”).
- **Optional**: “Feel free to forward to another dad who’s throttling…”

---

## ✅ Quick Checklist Before Sending
- [ ] Does it open with **raw pain** (my failure/chaos) instead of a systems metaphor?
- [ ] Is the tactic framed as **“what I built because I was screwing up”** rather than “deploy this protocol”?
- [ ] Did I include at least one **“still figuring it out / I still slip”** line?
- [ ] Is the newsletter ask **relational** (“if this hit home…”) instead of transactional?
- [ ] Would a tired dad feel **seen** and want more, or just feel lectured?
