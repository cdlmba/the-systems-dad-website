## ✍️ 4. Prose & Copy Standards (Substack Long-Form Adaptation)

Long-form Substack essays use **Narrative Rhythm with a Strict Jargon Ban**. They are decoupled from the rigid 15-word sentence limit of short-form social media, but strictly enforce all clarity and anti-jargon gates:

1. **The Banned Swap Table**:
   - Zero consulting buzzwords (`operational lanes` → `who owns what`, `async` → `written update`, `leverage` → `use it`, `bandwidth` → `time`, `deliverables` → `tasks/the work`, `systemic friction` → `the broken step`).

2. **The Wikipedia AI Signals Purge**:
   - No significance puffery (*"marks a pivotal moment," "sets the stage for"*).
   - No negative parallelisms (*"Not X, but Y"*). State positive facts directly.
   - No dangling *-ing* editorial gloss at sentence endings.
   - Zero banned AI words (*delve, tapestry, crucial, robust, landscape, bolstered, intricate, foster*).

3. **Physical Anchors**:
   - Every essay must feature tangible, touchable domestic objects: kitchen counter, coffee mug, mudroom coat hooks, car keys, laptop hinge, paper calendar.

4. **The Orwell Protocol**:
   - Never use a metaphor, simile, or figure of speech you are used to seeing in print.
   - Never use a long word where a short one will do.
   - If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
   - Never use the passive where you can use the active.
   - Never use a foreign phrase, scientific word, or jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
