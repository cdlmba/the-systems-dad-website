# Markdown Conflict Resolution Scripts

Use these prompts to navigate "logistics friction" and domestic "Administrative Drift" without the emotional drain.

A Strategic Lead knows that the "heart" of the house requires healthy "gears." These scripts help you fix the gears while protecting the heart.

### **The "Gears vs. Heart" Reframer**

Use this when a discussion about the schedule or household tasks starts to feel like an emotional conflict.

> We are experiencing some friction regarding [HOUSEHOLD ISSUE]. 
> 
> Help me draft a response to my spouse that separates the **Gears** (the logistics) from the **Heart** (our relationship). 
> 
> Follow the **Systems Dad** principles:
> 1. **Apply Transactional Analysis**: Help me stay in the "Adult" state—objective, calm, and collaborative. Avoid "Parent" (critical) or "Child" (defensive) reactions.
> 2. **Propose a Systemic Fix**: Instead of blaming, suggest a shared "Information Hub" or a "Weekly Sync" to solve it for good.
> 3. **Tone**: Upbeat and encouraging. Use plain language. Be practical and humble.
> 4. **Format**: Short sentences. No em-dashes. Active voice.

### **The "Validation & Leverage" Script**

Use this when your spouse is feeling "smothered" by the schedule or chores.

> My spouse is feeling overwhelmed by [ISSUE]. 
> 
> Draft a validating and supportive message that uses **Margin-First Productivity** principles:
> 1. **Acknowledge the Capacity Drain**: Validate that the current workload is stealing their margin.
> 2. **Offer Leverage**: Suggest one thing I can **Automate** or **Own** right now to buy back 2 hours of their time today.
> 3. **Protect the Connection**: Shift the focus from "who does what" to "how we win as a team."
> 4. **Tone**: Helpful big-brother. Sincere and humble. No jargon.

---

> [!TIP]
> **Systems Dad Pro-Tip**: If it starts to turn into a deep emotional talk, stop. Schedule a date night. Keep the Sync about the gears.
