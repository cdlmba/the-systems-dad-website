# Conflict Analysis: LinkedIn Voice Rules

### 🔴 Conflict 1: Staccato Brevity vs. Smooth Pacing

**Rule 6** says: *"Aim for under 100 words. Short, declarative, staccato sentences."*

**Rule 10** says: *"Do NOT chop paragraphs into single sentences for dramatic effect; combine mini-aphorisms so the narrative flows like a spoken observation."*

These were directly opposed. A 100-word post almost by necessity requires short staccato cuts, but Rule 10 explicitly banned that technique as "broetry." **Resolution:** We have relaxed the rigid 100-word limit in Rule 6 to prevent cutting the narrative too close to the bone, while explicitly allowing short, single-sentence staccato pacing in Rule 10 as long as it avoids repetitive "broetry" rhythm.

---

### 🔴 Conflict 2: Manifesto Rallying Cry vs. NO-BS Cut

**Rule 5** says: *"End with a definitive, hard-hitting rallying cry (e.g., 'The age of waiting is over')."*

**Rule 8** says: *"Do not use 'Keynote Aphorisms' or LinkedIn bumper stickers."*

"The age of waiting is over" **is** a keynote aphorism by Rule 8's own definition. These two rules cannot coexist as written. The close we want is closer to a **peer manifesto** than a bumper sticker — but the rule doesn't currently draw that line. **Resolution:** Rule 5 has been updated to explicitly allow for a "manifesto-like statement" as a rallying cry, while Rule 8's prohibition against "Keynote Aphorisms" remains in effect. This clarifies that the close should feel like an empowering call to action among peers (manifesto) rather than a generic, preachy bumper sticker (aphorism).

---

### 🟡 Conflict 3: Anecdotes in the Body vs. Manifesto Close

**Rule 9** says: *"The final line must always be the universal, hard-hitting coffee-test takeaway."*

**Rule 5** now says: *"End with a manifesto rallying cry."*

These are slightly different targets. A coffee-test axiom ("If you hold all the answers, you're the bottleneck") is observational and wry. A manifesto close ("The age of waiting is over") is declarative and identity-driven. Rule 9 still references the old coffee-test standard we replaced. It's a dangling contradiction.

**Resolution:** Rule 9 has been updated to reflect the new standard. Instead of mandating a "coffee-test takeaway," it now states that specific personal anecdotes belong in the body as evidence, while the final line serves as the manifesto rallying cry. This aligns Rule 9 with the updated Rule 5 and removes the contradiction.

---

### 🟡 Conflict 4: Hook Specificity vs. Hook Negation

**Rule 2** says: *"Open with a highly specific, grounded failure."*

**Rule 11** says: *"A highly effective pattern is to negate a common productivity trope."*

These aren't always compatible. A hook negation is often abstract by nature ("Waking up at 5:00 AM doesn't fix..."). A grounded failure is specific and situational. Right now Rule 2 reads as a hard requirement, while Rule 11 is framed as optional — but the framing should be clearer so the agent doesn't default to the wrong one.

**Resolution:** Rule 2 and Rule 11 have been consolidated into a single rule (now Rule 2) that explicitly allows for either (A) a Hook Negation or (B) a specific, grounded failure scenario. This clarifies that both approaches are equally valid and empowers the agent to choose the best opening for the specific post.
