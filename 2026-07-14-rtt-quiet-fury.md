# RTT: The Quiet Fury

*Real Talk Tuesday — July 2026*

---

### The Story

It usually happens in the driveway. Or right before you turn the doorknob after a long day.

It's a sigh.

Not a tired sigh. A pressurized one. Like a valve releasing steam.

You aren't angry at your wife. You aren't angry at your kids. You love them. You wanted this life.

But underneath all of it — the bill-paying, the running around, the logistics of keeping a family alive — there is a low-grade hum of frustration. A quiet fury.

Every hour is accounted for. Every ounce of your energy is budgeted. You walk in the door carrying the **Work Residue** from the day — still triaging a Slack thread — and when someone needs *just one more thing*, you feel something snap.

You snap at the kid. You give a short answer to your wife. You withdraw into your phone and become a **Ghost Dad** at your own dinner table.

And then the guilt hits. *What is wrong with me? I have a great life. Why am I so mad?*

So you push the fury down. Because a good man isn't supposed to feel resentful about the life he chose.

But pushed-down things don't disappear. They just go to the basement and start running the house from the dark.

---

### The Gear

We think we invented this feeling. Like it's some modern defect that came with smartphones and 60-hour weeks.

It's not.

Look at 1 Kings 19. Elijah the prophet just came off the biggest victory of his life. He stood down 400 false prophets. He proved God was real. He won.

And after the victory, a single threat breaks him.

He runs into the wilderness. He leaves his servant behind. He needs to disappear. He finds a tree, sits in the dirt, and says:

*"It is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life."*

A man who has given every single thing he has, and just can't carry the weight of being "the guy" for one more day.

Here's what gets me though —

He doesn't rebuke him. He doesn't call him ungrateful. He doesn't say, "Look at all the blessings you have, you coward."

He lets Elijah sleep. He sends an angel to feed him. The angel comes back a second time:

*"Arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you."*

God didn't tell Elijah he was weak. He told him the weight he was trying to carry alone was mathematically impossible.

---

### The Move

The quiet fury isn't a character flaw. It's your soul telling you something's broken.

When you feel the pressure building, you can keep pushing it down until it leaks out as passive-aggression toward your wife and impatience with your kids. Or you can name it.

Three moves:

**1. Name the leak.** Stop pretending you're just "tired." Admit that it costs you something to carry all of this. Acknowledging the cost doesn't mean you regret the choice. It just means you're being honest.

**2. Stop isolating.** Elijah's fury thrived because he was convinced he was the only one. *"I, even I only, am left."* You need another man who understands the exact pressure you're under. The fury grows in the dark.

**3. Act like a Steward, not a Sovereign.** You don't own this life. You're just looking after it for the King. Do your job, build the systems to protect your presence, and then let it go. You can't carry all of it.

The fury loses its power the second you stop pretending you're supposed to be bulletproof.

You aren't.

Then get back up.
