y2="14"></line></svg></button></div></div></div></a></figure></div><p></p><h2><strong>When Getting Close Isn&#8217;t Control</strong></h2><p>Here&#8217;s the problem. We&#8217;ve turned &#8220;micromanagement&#8221; into corporate kryptonite. Say it in a meeting and watch everyone recoil. But Fadell argues there&#8217;s a massive difference between suffocating your team and knowing your product cold.</p><p>Traditional micromanagement? That&#8217;s pure insecurity. Constant monitoring. Zero trust. Every decision funneled through you because you can&#8217;t imagine anyone else getting it right.</p><p>The damage is real. Innovation dies first. Why pitch new ideas when &#8220;different&#8221; just means &#8220;wrong&#8221;? Your team learns to wait for your blessing before typing a single email. And your best people? They leave. Always do.</p><h2><strong>The Zoom In</strong></h2><p>Fadell calls his approach &#8220;Zooming In.&#8221; It&#8217;s not about controlling people. It&#8217;s about understanding the work.</p><p>When you Zoom In, you&#8217;re asking why. Why this vendor? Why 200ms latency instead of 50? You&#8217;re not hunting for mistakes. You&#8217;re teaching people how you think.</p><p>He&#8217;s especially pointed about new hires. Stay close for the first two months. Not because you don&#8217;t trust them. Because they need context. Your systems have quirks and patterns they can&#8217;t see yet.</p><p>&#8220;Examining the product in great detail and caring deeply about the quality of what your team is producing is not micromanagement.&#8221;</p><p>That&#8217;s the line. Fadell wants you on that line.</p><h2><strong>The Line You Don&#8217;t Cross</strong></h2><p>So when does caring become controlling?</p><p>Check your intent. Are you dictating solutions or guiding discovery? Big difference.</p><p>Red flags: You&#8217;re constantly redoing their work yourself. You demand detailed reports that only feed your anxiety. You won&#8217;t let anyone make decisions, even tiny ones.</p><p>Real micromanagement comes from a trust gap. And that gap? Usually your fault. You either hired wrong or you&#8217;re too scared to let go.</p><h2><strong>The Zoom Out</strong></h2><p>The whole point of Zooming In is to eventually Zoom Out.</p><p>You do that by making &#8220;great&#8221; crystal clear. Your team can&#8217;t hit a target they can&#8217;t see. Define success so precisely that they know when they&#8217;ve nailed it.</p><p>Then match tasks to people. Give them projects where failing won&#8217;t sink the ship but will teach them plenty.</p><p>Build safety. Let them own the how while you stay locked on the why.</p><h2><strong>The Real Skill</strong></h2><p>Leadership isn&#8217;t staying out of the details nor is it drowning in them. It&#8217;s knowing when to move up the ladder of abstraction.</p><p>Dive into a line of code when quality&#8217;s at risk. Pull back when they&#8217;re crushing it. Rise up to set the next direction.</p><p>Next time someone calls you a micromanager, or you feel the pull to dig deep, ask yourself one thing: Am I controlling this or building it?</p><p>Your answer matters more than you think.</p>
