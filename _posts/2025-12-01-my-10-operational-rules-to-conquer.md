I refer to these daily to keep my life simple.</p><div><hr></div><h3><strong>1. Sacred Space: The Digital Fortress</strong></h3><blockquote><p><strong>The Principle:</strong> Keep your tools at the ready so inspiration has no friction.</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> Keep ONE THING on your screen. Use separate virtual desktops and ruthlessly control notifications.</p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Eliminates digital noise and reduces switching costs.</p><p></p></li></ul><h3><strong>2. Keep a List: Your Past and Future</strong></h3><blockquote><p><strong>The Principle:</strong> Your list is your past and future; carry it at all times.</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> Centralize all action items into a single, trusted system. Maintain the <strong>Three-Tier List:</strong> Today (3 High-Impact Tasks), This Week, and This Quarter.</p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Provides radical clarity on immediate and strategic priorities.</p></li></ul><h3><strong>4. Standard procedure is the master.</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> Every recurring task must be codified into a <strong>template</strong> or SOP (Template Mandate). Be On Time: Focused Presence</strong></h3><blockquote><p><strong>The Principle:</strong> Respect the clock, both for arrivals and sustained focus.</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> Being &#8220;on time&#8221; means being mentally present. Use the <strong>Mute Mandate</strong> in virtual meetings and <strong>Protect the Block</strong> by scheduling mandatory deep work time.</p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Restores personal agency and makes synchronous time effective.</p></li></ul><h3><strong>6. I Understand: End Ambiguity</strong></h3><blockquote><p><strong>The Principle:</strong> If you understand, say &#8220;I Understand.&#8221; If not, say &#8220;I Don&#8217;t Understand.&#8221;</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> Use the <strong>Readback Protocol</strong> for all critical items: the recipient must reply with <strong>&#8220;I understand: [Brief summary of the agreed action/decision].&#8221;</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Eliminates guesswork and prevents major rework/misalignment.</p><p></p></li></ul><p class="button-wrapper" data-attrs="{&quot;url&quot;:&quot;https://christopherlynn.substack.com/?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share&quot;,&quot;text&quot;:&quot;Share Leading with Intent&quot;,&quot;action&quot;:null,&quot;class&quot;:null}" data-component-name="ButtonCreateButton"><a class="button primary" href="https://christopherlynn.substack.com/?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share"><span>Share Leading with Intent</span></a></p><p></p><h3><strong>7. Sent Does Not Mean Received: The Confirmation Loop</strong></h3><blockquote><p><strong>The Principle:</strong> Always obtain a receipt or proof of delivery to the intended recipient.</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> Implement <strong>Confirmation Loops</strong>. For mission-critical communications, require a mandatory follow-up confirmation (e.g., &#8220;Please confirm receipt by 5 pm Central Time today&#8221;). Ensure a <strong>Digital Paper Trail</strong>.</p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Formalizes decision-making and closes the loop on high-risk items.</p></li></ul><h3><strong>8. Use the Plan Tomorrow, Today method and define the <strong>Tomorrow 3</strong> list (3 High-Impact Tasks for the next day) before logging off. Ensure all progress is documented (Pass It On).</p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Guarantees you start the next day in flow state, eliminating decision fatigue.</p></li></ul><p class="button-wrapper" data-attrs="{&quot;url&quot;:&quot;https://christopherlynn.substack.com/subscribe?&quot;,&quot;text&quot;:&quot;Subscribe now&quot;,&quot;action&quot;:null,&quot;class&quot;:null}" data-component-name="ButtonCreateButton"><a class="button primary" href="https://christopherlynn.substack.com/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><p></p><h3><strong>9. Procrastinate: Procrastination is a System Signal</strong></h3><blockquote><p><strong>The Principle:</strong> Sometimes, you have to delay to think about what&#8217;s most important.</p></blockquote><ul><li><p><strong>The Digital Mandate:</strong> When repeatedly avoiding a task, <strong>analyze the friction</strong> (Is the template wrong? Is the objective unclear?).</p></li><li><p><strong>Effect:</strong> Transforms avoidance from a time sink into a valuable diagnostic tool, revealing process errors or goal misalignment.</p><p></p></li></ul><h3><strong>10. Focus on the <strong>Daily Show-Up</strong> instead.
