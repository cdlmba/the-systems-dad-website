### The Room of Eggshells

"Dad, did you hear me?"

I looked at my daughter. She was holding a drawing she colored. Her eyes were searching my face. 

She had been standing there for two minutes. 

My body was sitting at the table. My mind was still in the VPs' sync meeting. I was mentally triaging a database lag and worry-drafting a Slack message.

I gave her a distracted nod. "That's nice, honey."

She didn't smile. She just folded the paper, put it in her pocket, and walked away. 

She had already learned to test the air around me. Walking on eggshells to avoid the heavy cloud of work residue I brought home.

I wasn't just a ghost. I was a burden. 

---

### The Blink

That night, I experienced *The Blink*. 

It’s that moment of sudden clarity where you look down the road and realize you are building a ghost legacy. 

I looked at our Saturday Dot chart. Out of 936 Saturdays we get before they leave the house, my oldest was past dot 600. 

A hard truth hit me. It wasn't about work-life balance or personal optimization. It was about who owns the system. 

**I do not own my time, my family, or my career. I’m just looking after them for the King.**

If I run a tight ship at work, but let my home run on fumes, I’m failing the most important job I have. I was stealing from my kids by bringing my unresolved workday through the front door.

---

### The Front Door Lag

I couldn't just tell myself to be better. Willpower is a bad system. I needed a real routine. 

I built a simple shutdown rule at 4:45 PM. 

Before I shut my laptop, I use a fast AI audit. It scans my Slack, lists my open loops, and drafts my end-of-day handoff. By 5:15 PM, my head is clear. I don't have to carry work home in my head.

I call this cutting the carryover. 

*   **Zero Carryover (0–5 mins)**: You walk in the door, kiss your wife, and you're actually there.
*   **High Lag (30–60 mins)**: You're nodding along at dinner, but you're really thinking about that database lag.
*   **Ghost Mode**: You never leave the office. You are a ghost at your own table.

I still mess this up some days. I still feel the urge to check my phone at 6 PM. But this simple routine gives me the first 10 minutes back. And those 10 minutes change the whole night.

The dots are slipping away. How much lag are you bringing through your front door?
