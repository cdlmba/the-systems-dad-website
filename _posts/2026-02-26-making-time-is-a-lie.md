y2="14"></line></svg></button></div></div></div></a></figure></div><p></p><h2><strong>The Problem With &#8220;Making Time&#8221;</strong></h2><p>The whole concept is a trap. When we believe we can generate more hours, we set ourselves up for burnout. We&#8217;re trying to control something we can&#8217;t control. Time doesn&#8217;t bend to our will. We live by the clock, and the clock wins every time.</p><p>Then comes the guilt. When we fail to &#8220;make time,&#8221; we assume something&#8217;s wrong with us. Maybe we&#8217;re not disciplined enough. Maybe we&#8217;re lazy.</p><p>The real problem?</p><p><strong>Our approach is broken.</strong></p><p>It&#8217;s a hamster wheel. You run faster, optimize harder, grind longer. Nothing changes. You just get more tired.</p><p>Time isn&#8217;t a resource you produce. It&#8217;s a fixed allowance you allocate.</p><h2><strong>What It Means to Steward Time</strong></h2><p>Stewarding means taking care of something valuable that&#8217;s been entrusted to you. It means responsibility, thoughtfulness, and planning ahead. A steward often has legal responsibility.</p><p><strong>Do you treat your time like it&#8217;s a legal weight?</strong></p><p>You have 168 hours each week. That&#8217;s it. Your power isn&#8217;t in creating more hours. It&#8217;s in deciding how to use the ones you have.</p><p>Think of time like money. You&#8217;ve got a budget. Where are you spending it? Are you making conscious choices, or just letting it drain away on autopilot?</p><h2><strong>How to Reallocate Your Time</strong></h2><p>Start with an audit. Track where your time actually goes for three or four days. No judgment, just data. You&#8217;ll spot the leaks fast. Social media scrolling. Meetings that could&#8217;ve been emails. Default activities you do because you&#8217;ve always done them.</p><p>Now comes the hard part: figuring out what matters. What aligns with your real goals and values? What&#8217;s genuinely important versus just urgent?</p><p>This is where you learn to say no. Protecting your time budget means turning down requests that don&#8217;t serve your priorities. It feels uncomfortable at first. It gets easier.</p><p>Here&#8217;s how to reallocate strategically. Cut low-value activities to create space for high-value ones. Batch similar tasks together instead of context-switching all day. Stack things together to maximize efficiency.</p><p>Automate or delegate whatever you can. Design your ideal week in advance, blocking out time for what matters before the urgent stuff fills every gap.</p><h2><strong>Rest Isn&#8217;t Optional</strong></h2><p>Rest is part of your time budget, not something you fit in if there&#8217;s room left over.</p><p>Your body and brain need downtime: sleep, mental breaks, actual rest. The hustle-harder mentality is a con. Pushing through exhaustion doesn&#8217;t make you productive. It makes you slow and stupid.</p><p>Schedule rest like you schedule meetings. Decide what rest means for you. Sometimes it&#8217;s Netflix. Sometimes it&#8217;s a walk. Sometimes it&#8217;s staring at the ceiling for twenty minutes because your brain needs to process nothing.</p><p>Rest compounds. When you&#8217;re rested, you focus better, think clearer, and get more done in less time. Skipping rest to work more is like refusing to refuel your car because you&#8217;re in a hurry.</p><p>Take <em><strong>one day a week</strong></em> for non-productive activity (I call it Sabbath-maxxing). It doesn&#8217;t have to be religious. It just has to be real. Recharge so you can steward the rest of your week better.</p><h2><strong>The Freedom in Limits</strong></h2><p>Accepting that time is finite is liberating, not depressing.</p><p>You stop feeling guilty about not doing everything. You start making intentional choices. You build a sustainable life, not just temporarily productive.</p><p>You trade reactive busyness for conscious decisions about what deserves your hours.</p><h2><strong>Stop Trying to Defy Physics</strong></h2><p>You will never make more time. But you can become an exceptional steward of the time you have.</p><p>It&#8217;s a shift from frantic creation to deliberate curation. Audit your hours. Align them with what matters. Protect the moments that let you recharge.</p><p>Try this for one week: stop saying &#8220;I need to make more time.&#8221; Ask yourself instead, &#8220;How can I better steward the time I have today?&#8221;</p><p>The life you want isn&#8217;t waiting for you to create more time. It&#8217;s waiting for you to manage what you&#8217;ve already been given.</p>
