He leveraged what he already had and put it out there. Then he got signed to another deal.</p><p>This lesson isn&#8217;t just for cut quarterbacks angling for a playoff spot. It&#8217;s for all of us, no matter where we are in our careers.</p><p>What I&#8217;ve learned writing online more consistently: it&#8217;s a series of shots. Some work. Some don&#8217;t. But you&#8217;ve got to show up in public.</p><p>The platform you choose first doesn&#8217;t matter as much as showing up.</p><h2><strong>Congratulations, you&#8217;re now part of the creator economy</strong></h2><p>I&#8217;ve talked to several clients about the job search process. It&#8217;s brutal out there. Most are where I&#8217;ve been: not publishing, not connecting, relying on skills alone to carry them forward.</p><p>Go back to Ben. One video showed him in a flag football game, tossing touchdowns like it was nothing. You&#8217;re probably not getting national broadcast cameras on you. But you can draw attention to your skillset by talking about how you bring magic to the process.</p><p>The craft of what you do reveals your thinking.</p><p>Let&#8217;s talk about publishing online. You can publish anywhere. Even your Facebook account, if you want.</p><p>Instead of just sharing about your favorite show, apply a lesson that connects to what you do. Skip the political debate and challenge assumptions in your industry instead.</p><h2><strong>But what if you love your current role?</strong></h2><p>You should be posting and connecting too.</p><p>Ben has two solid pieces of his brand. He didn&#8217;t need to audition for more football. But he knows it draws attention for his work with TRUE BRVND and his media opportunities.</p><p>What does this mean in your current role? More opportunity to show what you know. Sharpen your communication skills. Build authority alongside your confidence. Open up new skills: writing, speaking, marketing, sales.</p><p>For me, writing online has opened up new connections. Not just in my field, but people who&#8217;ve shaped me even more.</p><p>A non-exhaustive list: <a href="https://tylertodt.substack.com/">Tyler Todt</a>. <a href="https://www.leaderunlock.com/">Chris Antonelli</a>. <a href="https://creativityandculture.com/">Paul Bullard</a>. <a href="https://x.com/Dr_J4">Justin Jones</a>. <a href="https://x.com/caefurd">Caleb Efurd</a>. <a href="https://x.com/bldgodlymanhood">Ben Mitchell</a>.</p><h2><strong>Lessons from building in public</strong></h2><p>Being visible is currency. Once you catch attention, keep it. Ben wasn&#8217;t always visible. He was drafted out of James Madison University. Not exactly the limelight.</p><p>Be seen. Show up.</p><p>Show up and be authentic. People don&#8217;t need you to recycle others&#8217; stories. They need to hear from you. You might think what you know isn&#8217;t unique. It might not be. Show up for others in comments and reposts. Celebrate big accomplishments and milestones. Say &#8220;we&#8221; ten times more than &#8220;me.&#8221;</p><h2><strong>Your tool for publishing in public</strong></h2><p>Here&#8217;s how to get started being more public.</p><p><strong>Arena:</strong> What sport and court are you playing? Ben&#8217;s is football and reaching people interested in sports fashion. What&#8217;s yours?</p><ul><li><p><strong>Long-form:</strong> Substack, X articles (for X Premium), Medium, and LinkedIn articles are great for publishing long-form content. Deep dives show trust and authority.</p></li><li><p><strong>Short-form:</strong> One of my favorites. X/Twitter posts and threads, LinkedIn posts, Instagram carousels. Quick hits with a tease for more.</p></li><li><p><strong>Visual/audio:</strong> Lots of great opportunities. YouTube, podcasts, TikTok. You can use free or cheap tools to edit, polish, and publish across platforms.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Play to Run:</strong> What&#8217;s your value? How will you score consistently?</p><p>Skills highlights? Original opinion or analysis? How-to guides? Public questions or polls to draw discussion? Overview of a project or creation?</p><p><strong>Style:</strong> How are you going to show up? Gunslinger? Precise?</p><ul><li><p>Either way, you want a clear, compelling story. It should all come together.</p></li><li><p>Hooks matter. Whether you&#8217;re a trusted, predictable voice or a little edgy, there&#8217;s got to be something to pull the audience toward you.</p></li><li><p>Realize you&#8217;re always learning. The best writers still tweak their content.</p></li></ul><p><em><strong>Just start.</strong></em> I woke up one day and started publishing on Substack. I didn&#8217;t need permission. I just needed to start.</p><p><strong>Throw the Ball:</strong> Get on the field and do it.</p><ul><li><p>You&#8217;re going to get in your head. Keep throwing spirals. Keep trying.</p></li><li><p>If you&#8217;re 80% of the way there, you&#8217;re actually 100% of the way there.</p></li><li><p>Set a public commitment. Committing in public makes you responsible. For me, it&#8217;s publishing this every Thursday. Even on holidays. It requires a system.</p></li></ul><h2><strong>Talk with the fans, players, and coaches.</strong></h2><ul><li><p><strong>Fans:</strong> You&#8217;ll get some people to follow you. Know when to ignore the haters, but otherwise learn and grow with your commenters.</p></li><li><p><strong>Players:</strong> Other folks similar to your niche. Learn from them. Have friendly competition that grows you both. I reframed from &#8220;This guy is already doing what I&#8217;m doing&#8221; to &#8220;We can learn from each other.&#8221;</p></li><li><p><strong>Coaches:</strong> Larger accounts with more influence. Study what they&#8217;re doing well. Reach out. Learn.</p></li></ul><p>None of this happens unless you show up.</p><p>I was just listening to Mike Cernovich speak at Scott Adams&#8217; live-streamed celebration of life service. He talked about showing up in Scott&#8217;s comments. Mike&#8217;s a published author with a large social media account. If he&#8217;s finding mentors and showing up, we&#8217;ve got plenty of opportunities to do that.</p><p>Ben DiNucci will get more sales and speaking opportunities by shooting his shot in public.</p><p>I&#8217;m piecing together my livestreams and podcasts to work more directly with founders ready to launch. I&#8217;m pulling it all together in public. I&#8217;m learning skills online that help me spot opportunities at work.</p><p>It all works together.</p><h2><strong>Quick thoughts before we wrap up</strong></h2><p>&#8220;I still don&#8217;t know. This sounds risky.&#8221;</p><p>Everyone starts somewhere. The illusion of overnight success is just that. Your unique perspective has value. Focus on contribution, not perfection. Build your publishing muscle over time.</p><p>&#8220;Okay, I&#8217;m willing to try. This could be fun.&#8221;</p><p>Expect unexpected career pivots. Build a strong professional network organically. Develop new skills: writing, presentation, and critical thinking. Experience the satisfaction of creating and sharing.</p><h2><strong>Your move</strong></h2><p>Ben DiNucci didn&#8217;t just get a job. He built a blueprint for modern opportunity. He showed us that the best way to secure your future isn&#8217;t to wait for an invitation. It&#8217;s to build your own stage.</p><p>The Creator Economy isn&#8217;t just for influencers. It&#8217;s for you. Anyone with a unique perspective, a skill to share, or an idea to explore.</p><blockquote><p>Stop waiting for permission.
