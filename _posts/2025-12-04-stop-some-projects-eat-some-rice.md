href="https://unsplash.com">Unsplash</a></figcaption></figure></div><p></p><h1><strong>The Four Pillars of RICE</strong></h1><h2><strong>R = Reach: Who Will Feel the Ripple?</strong></h2><p>How many people will this project affect? Count customers, team members, collaborators, your whole community.</p><p>Broad impact means scalable growth. Launching a new feature touches customers. Updating an internal process affects your team. Writing a viral blog post reaches thousands of readers.</p><p>Score it simply. 100 people, 1,000 people, 10,000 people.</p><h2><strong>I = Impact: The Force Multiplier Effect</strong></h2><p>How significant is the positive outcome if this succeeds? Not all &#8220;reaches&#8221; pack the same punch.</p><p>Use a scale: Massive, High, Medium, Low, Minimal. Solving a critical pain point gets &#8220;Massive.&#8221; Improving user experience gets &#8220;High.&#8221; Minor bug fixes get &#8220;Low.&#8221;</p><p>Convert to numbers. 3x, 2x, 1x, 0.5x, 0.25x.</p><h2><strong>C = Confidence: Believing in the Win</strong></h2><p>How confident are you in your Reach and Impact estimates? This kills over-optimism dead.</p><p>Based on proven results? High confidence. Market research? Medium. Pure guess? Low.</p><p>Score it as percentages. 100%, 80%, 50%, 20%.</p><h2><strong>E = Effort: The Cost of the Prize</strong></h2><p>What&#8217;s the total work required? Include time, money, team bandwidth, mental energy.</p><p>Be specific. One week. Two days. Half a month. It reduces decision fatigue and mental clutter. You&#8217;ll focus better and waste less effort.</p><p>Most importantly, you can say &#8220;no&#8221; to low-RICE projects without guilt. Remember back in high school when you had to choose between studying for the chemistry test or the Spanish quiz. The test usually won out.</p><p>The RICE matrix does more than rank your projects. It rewires how you think about work itself.</p><p>When you start scoring everything through Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort, something clicks. Decision fatigue melts away because you&#8217;re not wrestling with gut feelings anymore. You&#8217;ve got numbers.</p><p>Your focus sharpens. Instead of bouncing between whatever feels urgent, you zero in on what actually makes a difference. It&#8217;s like switching from a flashlight to a laser.</p><p>You become ruthless with resource allocation. No more throwing good hours after bad projects. No more saying yes to everything because you can&#8217;t tell what matters.</p><p>Best of all? You can kill projects without guilt. When something scores a 2 and your top priority scores 47, the choice becomes obvious. You&#8217;re not being mean. You&#8217;re being smart.</p><p class="button-wrapper" data-attrs="{&quot;url&quot;:&quot;https://christopherlynn.substack.com/subscribe?&quot;,&quot;text&quot;:&quot;Subscribe now&quot;,&quot;action&quot;:null,&quot;class&quot;:null}" data-component-name="ButtonCreateButton"><a class="button primary" href="https://christopherlynn.substack.com/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><p></p><h1><strong>Watch Out for These Traps</strong></h1><p>The biggest mistake? Lying to yourself about the numbers. We love pumping up our pet projects&#8217; Reach and Impact while pretending they won&#8217;t take much Effort.</p><ul><li><p><strong>First trap:</strong> Going all out. Don&#8217;t do this. Start conservative. Better to underestimate and surprise yourself than crash into reality later.</p></li><li><p><strong>Second trap:</strong> scoring once and forgetting about it. Markets shift. Resources change. That project you rated highly three months ago might be worthless now. Rescore quarterly, minimum. Monthly if things move fast in your world.</p></li><li><p><strong>Third pitfall:</strong> analysis paralysis. You spend more time perfecting your RICE scores than actually doing the work.
