<h1>The Shadow Meeting</h1><p>Here&#8217;s something I&#8217;ve watched happen in almost every organization I&#8217;ve been part of.</p><p>The actual meeting ends.</p><p>And then the <strong>real</strong> meeting starts.</p><p>The Zoom call drops, people close their laptops, and five minutes later someone&#8217;s in your DMs: &#8220;Did you catch that?&#8221; or &#8220;I can&#8217;t believe he said that.&#8221; The sidebar conversations fire up. The group chat blows up. And suddenly, the work that should have happened in the room is happening in a dozen different side channels, minus context, minus the right people, and full of whatever emotions didn&#8217;t get aired out.</p><p>We call it gossip. But I&#8217;ve started calling it something else: <strong>shadow system behavior.</strong></p><p>And here&#8217;s the thing&#8212;shadow systems aren&#8217;t just annoying. They&#8217;re expensive. They slow your team down, they corrode trust quietly, and they have a way of following you home.</p><h2>Gossip is just technical debt with better PR</h2><p>You know what technical debt is. You take a shortcut now, you pay for it later&#8212;usually at the worst possible time.</p><p>Gossip works the same way.</p><p>Instead of spending five uncomfortable minutes saying &#8220;Hey, I disagree with that call and here&#8217;s why,&#8221; you spend five days doing the slow-burn version: venting to a trusted colleague, watching the tension build, letting small resentments stack up until someone eventually blows or quietly checks out.</p><p>The latency alone should bother you.</p><p>That feedback loop that could have closed in one conversation? It&#8217;s now running on a three-day delay across four people who don&#8217;t have full context. By the time anything gets resolved&#8212;if it does&#8212;you&#8217;ve burned twice the time and half the trust.</p><h2>The &#8220;nice&#8221; manager trap</h2><p>Here&#8217;s where I see it go sideways most often.</p><p>Someone on the team does something that creates friction. And the manager&#8212;you, maybe&#8212;decides to &#8220;let it go&#8221; because you don&#8217;t want to make things awkward. You&#8217;re being nice.</p><p>So you don&#8217;t say anything in the moment.</p><p>But the frustration doesn&#8217;t disappear. It just goes somewhere else. It leaks into a conversation with your peer. It shows up in how you respond to that person&#8217;s next email. And sometimes&#8212;more often than people admit&#8212;it follows you home.</p><p>You&#8217;re at the dinner table at 6:30 PM. Your kid is talking to you. And your mind is still replaying that meeting from 3 o&#8217;clock.</p><p>That&#8217;s the real cost. Not the workplace drama. It&#8217;s the margin it takes from you at home. y2="14"></line></svg></button></div></div></div></a></figure></div><h2>What I actually do about it: the Lighthouse Protocol</h2><p>I don&#8217;t have a magic fix. But I do have a rule set that&#8217;s made things a lot cleaner.</p><p>I call it the Lighthouse Protocol. Three simple rules:</p><p><strong>1. No triangulation.</strong></p><p>If you have a problem with someone, you talk to that person. You don&#8217;t come to me first. If you do come to me, I&#8217;m going to ask: &#8220;Have you told them yet?&#8221; That&#8217;s the whole conversation until the answer is yes.</p><p><strong>2. The 24-hour rule.</strong></p><p>Friction doesn&#8217;t get to sit. If something happened that needs a conversation, that conversation happens within 24 hours. Not next week. Not &#8220;when the timing is better.&#8221; 24 hours.</p><p><strong>3. The Reframer Prompt.</strong></p><p>When you&#8217;re fired up and the urge to vent is real&#8212;hit pause and drop this into your AI tool of choice first:</p><p><code>&#8221;I&#8217;m frustrated with [person/situation] because [reason]. Act as a senior Program Manager. Give me a 2-sentence direct feedback statement that focuses on the process failure, not the person&#8217;s character. Keep it professional and actionable.&#8221;</code></p><p>Nine times out of ten, what comes back is something you can actually say out loud to the person. That&#8217;s the move.</p><h2>Why this is a presence issue, not just a work issue</h2><p>When you clean up the feedback loops at work, something interesting happens at home.</p><p>You actually get to leave.</p><p>Not just physically, but mentally. When problems get addressed directly and quickly, they don&#8217;t have a reason to follow you into the car ride home, or the dinner table, or the Saturday morning when your kid wants you to play catch.</p><p>A team with clean communication doesn&#8217;t just ship faster.</p><p>It gives you your margin back.</p><p>Don&#8217;t let the shadow meeting steal your weekend.</p>
