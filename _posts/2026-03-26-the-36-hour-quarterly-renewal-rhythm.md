On a Friday in late March, it usually just feels like I&#8217;m failing everyone.</p><p>My morning routine starts to fray. I find myself snapping at my wife over something stupid, usually because I&#8217;m halfway through a spreadsheet in my head while she&#8217;s trying to tell me about her day. I&#8217;m tired of limping across the finish line.</p><p>If you&#8217;re a Systems Dad, you need a system for <strong>renewal</strong>, not just more ways to squeeze work out of your day. y2="14"></line></svg></button></div></div></div></a></figure></div><div><hr></div><h3>Friday: The Break</h3><p>I start at 6:00 PM. The goal is simple: kill the noise.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Log out of Slack.</strong> If I don&#8217;t explicitly log out, I&#8217;ll &#8220;just check&#8221; one more time. I also do a brain dump into my journal&#8212;every loose end goes on paper so it doesn&#8217;t haunt me at 2:00 AM.</p></li><li><p><strong>A &#8220;No Work&#8221; Dinner.</strong> This is harder than it sounds. No phones. No talk about projects. Just being there for my wife and kids.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bedtime Prayers.</strong> Don&#8217;t rush this. It&#8217;s the one time of day I can actually see what&#8217;s going on in my kids&#8217; heads.</p></li></ul><h3>Saturday Morning: The Audit</h3><p>I usually sleep in. Once I&#8217;m up and moving (a long walk is best), I sit down with a journal. No judgment, just honesty.</p><ul><li><p>What actually worked this quarter? Where did I lose my cool? How is my walk with God?</p></li><li><p>The most important part: I look at my wife. What signals have I been missing because I was too busy &#8220;leading&#8221;?</p></li></ul><h3>Saturday Afternoon: The Re-Launch</h3><p>I pick <strong>one</strong> big win for the next quarter. Not ten. One for work, one for my family (like a specific trip or date night), and one for my own growth.</p><p>Then I sit down with my wife. I share my plan, but mostly I just ask: <em>&#8220;What do you need from me for the next three months?&#8221;</em> Usually, her answer is the one thing I wasn&#8217;t even thinking about.</p><div><hr></div><h3>Stewardship, Not Selfishness</h3><p>This isn&#8217;t a &#8220;vacation.&#8221; It&#8217;s stewardship. You can&#8217;t lead your family if your tank is empty.</p><p>Consistency is better than perfection here. Some quarters, the kids are sick or the water heater breaks and the 36 hours gets squeezed. Do it anyway. Even a messy reset is better than none.</p><p>Check your calendar for the last weekend of the quarter. Block it off. Tell your wife. Let&#8217;s finish the next one strong.</p><p><strong>Systems Spotlight: &#128295;</strong><br><em>This week, I&#8217;m using the <a href="https://whop.com/christopher-lynn-systems-llc/the-weekly-retro-prompt">Weekly Retro Protocol</a> to catch loose ends before my reset starts. Try it out.</em></p><div><hr></div><p><strong>What&#8217;s your current &#8220;renewal rhythm&#8221;? Reply and let me know&#8212;I&#8217;d love to hear how you recharge.</strong></p>
