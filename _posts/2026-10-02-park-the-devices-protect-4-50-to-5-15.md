The meeting ends at 4:47. You answer one more message. Then another. You stand up, grab your keys, and your phone lights up on the way to the car.

You drive home rehearsing a reply. You pull in still rehearsing it. You walk in the door with a hello on your lips and a Slack thread in your head.

The workday didn't end. It just changed rooms.

Most of us treat the end of the workday as a moment. It isn't. It's a stretch of time, and if you don't decide what happens in it, the phone will decide for you.

## What is the 4:50 Protocol?

**The 4:50 Protocol is a 25-minute bridge from work to family, from 4:50 to 5:15.** You spend 10 minutes on a hard shutdown (4:50–5:00). Then you take 15 minutes to commute and clear your head (5:00–5:15), finishing with a 2-minute Driveway Reset. At the 5:15 Hard Stop you're home, and your devices go to a set spot.

In one line: **10 minutes to shutdown. 15 minutes to commute and clear your head.**

*Thanks to Ryan James Miller, who pushed me to package this.*

## Why it's 10 + 15

It's called the 4:50 Protocol because that's when it starts.

The window has two jobs, and they don't mix. The first job is closing work. That takes 10 minutes. The second job is getting from work to home with your attention pointed at home. That takes 15.

When you blur the two together, neither one gets done. You "shut down" while driving, which means you're still working, just with worse focus. Or you walk in the door to decompress, which means your family gets the decompression. Splitting the window gives each job its own time and a clear end.

## Block 1, 4:50–5:00: the 10-Minute Hard Shutdown

Ten minutes at the desk. Close the workday on purpose so it doesn't ride home with you. When the 10 minutes are up, the workday is done.

The rule that matters: it's 10 minutes, and it ends at 5:00. A shutdown that keeps stretching isn't a shutdown. It's more work.

## Block 2, 5:00–5:15: commute and clear your head

Fifteen minutes. The drive, the walk, or the train, with your head clear by the time you reach the door.

**5:00–5:13: Focus.** What you do here depends on you. The purpose doesn't. Use these minutes to focus: shift your attention off work and onto home. Audio is the recommended option (audio track coming soon).

**5:13–5:15: the Driveway Reset.** The last two minutes, in the car or on the doorstep, before your hand touches the door. Phone face-down. Name one thing you're looking forward to inside. That's it.

**If you work from home:** you still get the 15 minutes. Spend them reflecting quietly in your office before you leave it and walk into the house.

## 5:15: the Hard Stop. Park the devices

5:15 is the Hard Stop. You're home, at the door.

This is when the devices get parked. Not before. The phone rides with you through the commute for the audio and the Driveway Reset. Once you're home, it goes to its set spot. Not your pocket. Not the kitchen counter. Not "face down next to me."

Pick one spot and use it every day. The point is that the phone stops having a vote in your evening.

## What breaks the window

**The meeting that runs to 4:59.** If a call eats Block 1, the shutdown doesn't happen. It just gets skipped. Protect 4:50 like any other meeting you can't move.

**The phone that comes inside with you.** If it walks through the door in your pocket, the office still has a vote. Park it at 5:15.

**The commute that turns into calls.** Fifteen minutes on speakerphone isn't clearing your head. It's a second workday with a steering wheel. Use the block to focus.

**Skipping the Driveway Reset.** Pulling in and walking straight through the door with the last call still running in your head. Two minutes. Take them.

**What is the 5:15 Hard Stop?**
The time you're home, at the door. Your devices go to their set spot. It's the end of the 4:50 Protocol, not the start of your shutdown.

**What should I do during the 15-minute commute?**
Focus. Shift your attention from work to home. How you do that depends on you. Audio is the recommended option. Finish with the 2-minute Driveway Reset.

**When and where should I park my phone?**
At 5:15, when you get home. Keep it through the commute for audio. Park it in one set spot that isn't on you, the same spot every day.
