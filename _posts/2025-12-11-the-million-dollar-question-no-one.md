values, passions, and high-leverage problems you&#8217;re uniquely wired to solve. In research terms, this maps to what Herminia Ibarra calls the <strong>&#8220;working identity&#8221;</strong>, the version of yourself that emerges through action, not introspection (Harvard Business Review, <em>&#8220;The Authenticity Paradox,&#8221;</em> 2015).</p><p>Ibarra&#8217;s findings are blunt: <strong>young leaders discover their identity by doing.</strong></p><h3><strong>The Core Problem</strong></h3><p>Emerging leaders face enormous pressure to mimic, conform, or prematurely specialize. How to Become an Intentional Experimenter (AKA: How to Find Your Trunk)</strong></h1><h2><strong>A. Adopt a Hypothesis Mindset</strong></h2><p>Herminia Ibarra encourages leaders to <strong>&#8220;test possible selves&#8221;</strong> through small, reversible actions. Ask:</p><ul><li><p>&#8220;If I took on this project, what might I learn?&#8221;</p></li><li><p>&#8220;What version of me does this unlock?&#8221;</p></li></ul><p>Define success as learning, not outcomes.</p><h2><strong>B. Use Low-Risk, High-Learning Tactics</strong></h2><p><strong>Side Projects &amp; Internal Initiatives<br></strong>Research from MIT Sloan shows that employees who volunteer for cross-functional projects expand their promotable skillsets at nearly <strong>2x the rate</strong> of peers who stay in their lane (MIT Sloan, 2020).</p><p><strong>Borrowing Ideas<br></strong>Innovation scholarship repeatedly confirms that cross-pollination&#8212;importing an idea from one domain into another&#8212;is one of the most reliable sources of breakthrough innovation (Hargadon, <em>How Breakthroughs Happen</em>).</p><p><strong>Micro-Pilots<br></strong>Testing a process with a 3&#8211;5 person team for two weeks reduces risk while maximizing insight.</p><p><strong>Information Interviews<br></strong>According to LinkedIn&#8217;s 2022 Career Pathways Report, <strong>people who conduct six or more informational interviews are 56% more likely to identify a new career direction that fits their strengths.</strong></p><h2><strong>C. Master the Art of Failing Forward</strong></h2><p><strong>Debrief, Don&#8217;t Dwell<br></strong>After-action reviews (AARs) from the U.S. Army have been shown to increase team learning effectiveness by <strong>up to 38%</strong> (Harvard Business School, 2014).</p><p><strong>Reframe Failure as Data<br></strong>This aligns with Carol Dweck&#8217;s &#8220;growth mindset&#8221; findings: people who treat failures as information accelerate development.</p><h2><strong>D. Listen to Your Inner Compass</strong></h2><p>Pay attention to:</p><ul><li><p>Work that energizes you (Daniel Pink calls this &#8220;intrinsic motivation&#8221;).</p></li><li><p>Tasks you lose track of time doing (Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi&#8217;s flow research).</p></li><li><p>Problems you voluntarily return to.</p></li><li><p>Compliments that surprise you.</p></li></ul><p>These are roots revealing themselves.</p><div><hr></div><h1><strong>V. Addressing the Naysayers (and Your Inner Critic)</strong></h1><h3><strong>&#8220;I&#8217;ll look unfocused.&#8221;</strong></h3><p>Frame it as structured exploration. Research shows employees who articulate learning goals outperform those who define early fixed goals (HBR, 2011).</p><h3><strong>&#8220;My boss won&#8217;t let me.&#8221;</strong></h3><p>Micro-experiments embedded in existing work don&#8217;t trigger resistance. Demonstrate value with early wins.</p><h3><strong>&#8220;I don&#8217;t have time.&#8221;</strong></h3><p>McKinsey&#8217;s &#8220;Time Leadership&#8221; study: <strong>automating or eliminating just one recurring task frees ~12&#8211;20% of weekly capacity.</strong></p><h3><strong>&#8220;What if I make a mistake?&#8221;</strong></h3><p>Google&#8217;s Project Aristotle found psychological safety&#8212;and the willingness to make mistakes publicly&#8212;is the number one predictor of high-performing teams.</p><p></p><div class="subscription-widget-wrap-editor" data-attrs="{&quot;url&quot;:&quot;https://christopherlynn.substack.com/subscribe?&quot;,&quot;text&quot;:&quot;Subscribe&quot;,&quot;language&quot;:&quot;en&quot;}" data-component-name="SubscribeWidgetToDOM"><div class="subscription-widget show-subscribe"><div class="preamble"><p class="cta-caption">Christopher Lynn | The Systems Dad is a reader-supported publication.
