### The Ghost of Sunday Night

It was 10:45 PM on a Sunday. 

I was lying in bed, but my eyes were fixed on the ceiling. Next to me, my wife was breathing quietly, fast asleep. But my heart was pounding. I was mentally reviewing tomorrow’s sprint, pre-solving a tense meeting with a director, and checking Slack in my head.

I couldn't take it anymore. I quietly rolled out of bed, grabbed my laptop, and sat at the kitchen counter. *“Just a quick triage,”* I told myself. *“I'll get a head-start so tomorrow morning isn't a dumpster fire.”*

Two hours later, my eyes were burning. I had more anxiety, not less. And I had ruined whatever rest my weekend had left. 

I was working the Sunday Night Ghost Shift. Not because I loved the hustle, but because I was terrified of Monday morning. 

---

### Gating the Sabbath

We aren't built to carry the weight of our work 24/7. 

A hard truth hit me: **Rest is not a reward. It is a requirement. We don’t own our roadmaps or our careers. We are just caretakers.**

But you can't rest a brain that is still vibrating. If your Friday is a scramble of half-done tasks, your weekend will be haunted. You can't show up for your kids on Saturday if your mind is still in Jira.

To steward the weekend atmosphere for my family—to make sure I was actually throwing the football on Saturday instead of mentally debugging—I had to build a gate around my rest. 

Productivity isn't a volume game. It's a capacity game. 

---

### The Friday Afternoon Gate

I stopped fighting the Sunday night ghost shift. I built a simple gate on Friday afternoon instead.

Here is the exact Friday Reset checklist I run at 3:30 PM:

**1. The EOD Cleanout**
*   I use a simple AI prompt to scan my Slack channels and Jira boards. 
*   It pulls out the active action items and outstanding tasks from the week.

**2. The Monday Box**
*   I do not leave tasks floating. I block out exact times for them on my Monday calendar. 
*   By putting it in a box, my brain knows the work is safe to ignore until Monday morning. 

**3. The Shutoff**
*   I close the laptop at 4:30 PM. No "one last check." 
*   I remind myself: *"I worked hard. The rest is in the King's hands."*

I still mess this up. Some Fridays are absolute firefights, and I end up checking email on Saturday. But when I protect this hour, Monday breakfast is a family meal—not a meeting triage.
