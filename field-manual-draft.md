# The Systems Dad Field Manual — Draft Pass

## Chapter 1: The Ghost in the Machine

You run a tight ship at work. You’ve fixed big problems, managed huge budgets, and built teams that win. You know how to get things done. You're the guy they call when the project is off the rails and the pipeline is stalled. You are an operator.

But at 5:07 PM, when you pull into the driveway, the gears don't shift. You walk through the front door. The dog barks. The kids are yelling about something that happened at school. Your wife asks you a simple question about dinner. 

You give a short, clipped answer. 

You're physically in the house, but your brain is still writing that email to the VP of Engineering. You're thinking about the sprint review tomorrow. You are a ghost.

I know that feeling of the 'work loop' in your head that just won't close. I know the guilt of being checked out. I was the guy who had to answer every question at work, and I brought that same bottleneck energy home. I thought they were being respectful when the house got quiet after I walked in. They weren't. They were walking on eggshells.

This isn't a book about productivity. Productivity is for the office. This is a field manual about presence. 

If you want to stop being a ghost at your own dinner table, you don't need a life coach. You don't need a motivational speech. You need a system. A specific set of protocols that shut off the work brain and spin up the dad brain before you ever touch the front doorknob.

Welcome to the manual. Let's fix the pipes.

## Chapter 2: The Silent Killer of Your Ambition

You probably think burnout comes from working too many hours. It doesn't. 

Burnout comes from open loops.

An open loop is an unresolved decision, a pending conversation, or a loose end that your brain refuses to let go of. When you leave the office with 14 open loops—the code review that isn't finished, the passive-aggressive slack message you need to reply to, the architecture decision you haven't made—your brain keeps trying to close them.

This is why you're exhausted at 7 PM even if you didn't do any physical labor. Your brain is burning glucose trying to hold onto all these incomplete threads. 

When you walk into the kitchen with 14 open loops, your family doesn't get the best version of you. They get whatever margin is left over after the open loops have taken their cut. Usually, that's nothing but frustration and a short fuse.

We don't solve this by 'caring less' about work. That's a loser's game. We solve this by building a hard stop. We need a system that captures every open loop, dumps it into a trusted system, and tells your brain: 'It's safe to let this go until tomorrow at 8 AM.'

That system is the Evening Shutdown Protocol.
