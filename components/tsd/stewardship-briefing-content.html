Why it matters right now

My daughter stopped telling me about her day.

I didn't catch the exact moment it happened. I was physically here, but I was mentally still at work. My mind was still grinding on the previous week while she was trying to share her heart.

She learned what I was available for. She adjusted accordingly.

I realized I wasn't just dropping balls—I was failing as a steward of the 936 weeks I’ve been entrusted with.

We don't own our families. We are temporary caretakers called to lead them with excellence for a King. And the window of that stewardship is closing faster than we want to admit.

I built The Stewardship Briefing as a ritual to look up from the dashboard and close the gap.

Maybe you need it too.

I manage complex projects at work. I coordinate timelines and keep teams on track.

Then I get home—and I realized I was bringing that same work stress to the dinner table. My kids could sense the tension before I even sat down.

I thought my system was my memory. Bad plan.

When your plan is "I'll remember to do that tomorrow," you're nagging yourself for updates every hour. A slice of your brain is permanently occupied—which means you aren't actually home.

The three simple routines I built to fix it:

1. Household Hub: Stop being the only person who holds the information.
2. Sunday Sync: 15 minutes to clear out the noise before Monday.
3. The Shutdown Routine: Leaving work at your desk before dinner.

The result: I'm present for dinner. Mentally. Physically. Fully.

Every issue follows this format. Human first. Routine second. Action always.
