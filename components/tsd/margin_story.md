# The Logic of Being There
I spend my days fixing big problems for large companies. I’m good at it. I don't drop balls at work. But for a long time, I let work pings steal my dinner table.

I knew the feeling of that "work loop" in my head that just wouldn't close. I was in the house, but my brain was still at the office.

**I was the problem in my own home.**

I finally had to realize that "Being there" is the **result of a good plan.** You can't just "try harder" if you're carrying the weight of a thousand small house details in your head.

Making time isn't about doing more; it's about building the systems that let you **put the phone in the drawer** and actually hear what your family is saying. 

It’s about being there for the moments that matter, before they are gone.
