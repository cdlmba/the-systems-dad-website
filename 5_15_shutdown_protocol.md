# The 5:15 Shutdown

**How to actually leave work at work.**

It usually happens in the driveway. You put the car in park, but the loops are still running. 

You walk through the front door, but your mind is somewhere else. Secretly triaging Slack threads. Replaying meetings. You slip into ghost mode, and your family knows it. 

Willpower won't fix this. You need a better setup. This 10-minute routine is what I use to shut the work-brain off before I head home. Try it today and actually be home tonight.

## The Routine

### 1. Get it out of your head (5:05 PM)
- **What We Do:** Close out of Slack, grab a physical notepad, and write down every single open loop or pending task still bouncing around in your head.
- **Why We Do It:** You have to get the mess out of your head and onto paper. If you don't write it down, your brain will just keep chewing on it at the dinner table.

### 2. Lock it in the calendar (5:08 PM)
- **What We Do:** Pull up tomorrow’s calendar and block out the first 30 minutes of your morning to tackle the top two items from that list.
- **Why We Do It:** You need to tell your brain exactly when you're going to solve the problem. This is what actually kills the evening anxiety.

### 3. The final message (5:12 PM)
- **What We Do:** Drop one last message to the team: *"Signing off. If servers are on fire, text me. Otherwise, I will review in the morning."*
- **Why We Do It:** It sets a hard boundary and lets you off the hook for the rest of the night. 

### 4. Close the lid (5:15 PM)
- **What We Do:** Don't just let the laptop go to sleep—physically shut the lid. Take one deep breath and actually say it out loud: *"The work day is done. I am going home."* 
- **Why We Do It:** I know it sounds weird, but do it anyway. The physical action forces your brain to shift gears.

## Where guys mess this up

- **The Trap:** Writing the list but skipping the calendar block. Your brain is going to keep working on those problems all night if it doesn’t trust that you have time set aside to solve them tomorrow.
- **The Commute:** If you have a drive home, stop taking work calls in the car. Use the physical distance to enforce the mental distance.
